Research

How people respond to changing conditions, how choices are presented, and what those choices mean for well-being and sustainability.

Doctoral research

Weather Adversity in Tourism: Cognitive Appraisal, Coping Strategies and Tourist Decision-Making examines how tourists appraise and cope with adverse weather, and how those responses shape decisions and experiences.

PhD in Marketing and Tourism, Griffith University. Thesis submitted 2 October 2026 and under examination.

Master’s in Tourism, Fluminense Federal University, with a research exchange at the University of Girona. Visiting Scholar at the Isenberg School of Management, University of Massachusetts Amherst.

Adverse weather and tourist decision-making

I use interviews, focus groups and photo-ethnography to study how tourists and tourism managers respond to adverse weather. This work follows how travellers interpret disruptions, adapt their plans and sometimes reappraise an experience as special.

Read the paper on weather reappraisal →
Choice architecture and sustainable behaviour

My work examines behavioural nudges in tourism and people’s preferences for who controls online choice settings. I led data collection in Brazil for the international online choice architecture project.

Read the 26-country preprint →
Global Mental Wealth

I led the Brazilian data collection for a study of social, financial and emotional well-being involving 53,799 people across 92 countries.

Read the published study →
Applied tourism research

I contributed to a national rural tourism initiative supporting 14 tourism routes across Brazil.

Research methods

Scenario-based experiments, surveys, interviews, focus groups, photo-ethnography, systematic reviews and netnography.

Analysis includes reflexive thematic analysis, grounded theory, regression, ANOVA and MANOVA, covariance-based structural equation modelling, and topic modelling. I also use APIs for data collection and text analysis.
